# NYC 311 SLA Analysis — Findings & Recommendations

## Context
Analysis of 853,689 NYC 311 service requests (June–September 2026), examining 
resolution-time patterns across agencies, complaint types, and boroughs.

## Methodology note on SLA threshold
The dataset's due_date field was 99.6% missing, making it unusable as an SLA 
baseline. Resolution-time distribution was used instead: median = 4.41 hours, 
mean = 90.4 hours. The large gap between mean and median indicated a long tail 
of outlier complaints skewing the average. Three times the median (13.2 hours) 
was used as the working "breach" threshold. Overall breach rate: 39.42%.

## Finding 1 — Agency, not geography, drives delay
Borough-level breach rates ranged narrowly (35%–48.5%), ruling out location as 
a major factor. Agency-level rates told a different story: Department of 
Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) had a 98% breach rate, while NYPD — 
the highest-volume agency — had only 3%.

## Finding 2 — Housing-related complaint types are the core issue
Complaint types under HPD's jurisdiction (rodent, water leak, unsanitary 
conditions) had breach rates near 100%, confirming that agency-level delay is 
concentrated in a specific process area rather than distributed broadly.

## Recommendation
Resourcing and process improvements should target HPD's complaint-handling 
workflow specifically, not be distributed by borough. A geography-first 
resourcing strategy would not address the primary driver of delay found here.

## Limitations
- The 13.2-hour threshold is a statistically derived proxy, not an official 
  city SLA target
- Analysis covers 2.5 months; a full year of data would surface seasonal 
  effects (e.g., winter heating complaints) not visible here
- Staffing and budget data were not available, limiting root-cause analysis 
  beyond the complaint-type pattern
